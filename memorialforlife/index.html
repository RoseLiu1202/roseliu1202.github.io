A Memorial for the Origin of Life

2026
Sound sculpture proposal

Artist Statement

A Memorial for the Origin of Life is a proposed sound sculpture imagining a future in which technology has made human life indefinite. Situated in the waters of Penglai, China, a place entwined with ancient pursuits of immortality, the proposed sculpture becomes a memorial to natural birth and the intimate connection between body, life, and origin.

My work looks back at the present from an imagined future, asking what our technological ambitions reveal about our fears and desires. Technology is never neutral: it carries the politics, psychology, and aspirations of those who create it. I am particularly drawn to the pursuit of immortality, a desire that has moved through mythology, alchemy, science fiction, and biotechnology like a persistent whisper: what if we could live eternally? Why can’t we do better than nature?

A Memorial for the Origin of Life imagines a future in which the pursuit of immortality has transformed how life begins and ends. Consciousness uploading and digital copies promise existence beyond the biological body; cryonics holds bodies in suspension in the hope of future revival; and artificial wombs separate gestation from the maternal body. In the speculative world of this work, these ambitions have been realized, and natural birth has become obsolete. Taking the shape of a womb, the sound sculpture becomes a memorial to where we began: the intimate connection between mother and child, the discovery of the world through a changing body, and a relationship with life that is fluid, intuitive, and natural.

Inside the sculpture, visitors are invited to lie curled within its form, held by the sounds of their own heartbeat and the natural world around them. I hope this becomes a contemplative experience that returns them to the felt reality of their bodies.

In imagining what they would preserve forever, I hope they are quietly brought back to what they already have, and to what they might be running from. What are they unsatisfied with in this life? What are they still searching for? The desire for immortality is never about living forever. It is about what feels unfinished, unresolved, or unlived in the time we are given.
