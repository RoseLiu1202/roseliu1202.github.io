Sinking

2024
Performance-based photography

Artist Statement

This work is a self-shot photographic performance composed of five long-exposure images staged within a domestic kitchen. Using my own body as both subject and structural element, I examine how domestic space shapes identity and how inherited roles such as mother, daughter, and caretaker enter and reshape the psyche. Through repeated gestures at the sink, the camera captures the body in motion, allowing time to dissolve its edges until only blurred traces remain. The sink, cabinets, and counters act not simply as background but as a domestic architecture that holds the body even as it confines it.

The sequence engages with the quiet, cyclical labor embedded in home life and the emotional weight of intergenerational expectations. The partially disappearing nude figure reveals the erosion of the raw, unperformed self, worn down by repetition, caretaking, and the subtle demands of familial and gendered roles. Through the accumulation of time across each exposure, the work raises questions about what aspects of the self endure when identity is shaped by roles that precede personal desire.

Using the visual language of photographic time, including motion blur, repetition, and serial imagery, the piece transforms an ordinary interior into a site of psychological inquiry. It functions simultaneously as a self-portrait and a broader reflection on how domestic environments, inherited identities, and cultural expectations sculpt the interior landscape of the self.
